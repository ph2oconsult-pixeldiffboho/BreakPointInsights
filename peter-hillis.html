Peter Hillis

Founder and Principal

Chemist and water process scientist. Four decades in the water industry, from the bench to the board paper.

Photo of Peter Hillis
Career

Bench to board paper.

Peter began in a North West Water laboratory in 1981, analysing samples, investigating treatment problems and building an intake protection scheme against pollution incidents. That grounding in what water actually does, measured rather than assumed, has shaped everything since.

Through the 1990s he moved into research, first as a scientist running pilot trials to meet new regulatory requirements, then as Research Scientist and Research Associate managing programmes across membrane microfiltration, filtration and backwashing, Cryptosporidium, chemical mixing, disinfection by-products, coagulation, activated carbon, ozone and catchment management. He managed external research contracts and the technology-development budget, ran MSc and PhD studentships with universities, and presented the work internationally.

At United Utilities he carried that research into capital investment: managing the pilot-plant programme comparing direct filtration, high-rate clarification and membrane systems for lowest whole-life cost; setting the utility's process design standards and maintaining its process engineering competency; developing long-term asset plans and technology evaluation for successive regulatory investment periods; and representing the company to the Drinking Water Inspectorate and Environment Agency. He presented technical evidence to the national Badenoch and Bouchier committees on Cryptosporidium in drinking water.

Since 2011 he has been Technical Director for water and wastewater treatment at two of the region's largest engineering consultancies, AECOM and Aurecon, and ran his own independent practice, pH2O Consulting, between them. His recent work is dominated by technical challenge and assurance: process subject-matter expert to owners through early contractor involvement, value engineering lead on one of Australia's largest water treatment facilities, technical challenge lead on a $400M upgrade bundle, failure investigation, and technology and supplier readiness assessment for emerging biosolids processes.

BreakPoint is the written-down form of that career: an attempt to capture, test and transfer what four decades of treatment decisions have taught.
