BreakPoint Capacity.

Process capability assessment for water and wastewater treatment. We don’t ask only whether a plant can reach a flow. We determine the operating envelope within which it can reliably meet its treatment obligations, which constraint governs each edge, and how well each edge is known.

The problem

Five numbers that get confused.

Ask a plant what its capacity is and you get a number. Ask five people at the utility and you get five, each of them correct, because “capacity” is doing the work of five distinct concepts. Most capacity studies report one and never say which. The assessment reports all five, and the gaps between them are where the money is.

Nominal

Design flow: the figure on the drawings and in the licence. A statement of intent.

Literature-supported or Assumed
Theoretical

What unit calculations say each stage could handle under stated conditions, before coupled constraints.

Calculated
Demonstrated

What the plant has actually achieved and sustained while meeting its obligations, from the operating record. Almost never written down.

Measured
Constrained

The flow at which the first real-world limitation binds: solids, chemistry, hydraulics, equipment, control, people.

Calculated or Modelled, calibrated to record
Resilient

What can be sustained with a unit out, at tail water quality, or under a combination of adverse conditions.

Modelled, with stated scenarios

Capacity is an envelope, not a number.

A plant has a region, in the space of flow and influent condition, within which it can reliably meet its obligations. Different constraints govern different edges, and each edge is known to a different standard. Drawing that envelope is the real capacity study.

How we work

Two lenses, one envelope.

Neither design calculations nor historical data are sufficient alone. Each is used to challenge the other, differences are reconciled with operations, and the result is a single envelope that separates what is demonstrated from what is inferred, modelled or nominal.

Top-down: engineering analysis

Design basis, process criteria, drawings, equipment duties and control philosophy. Hydraulic checks, unit loadings, retention and contact time, mass balance, kinetics, N-1 capacity, interface review. Finds theoretical limits, missing redundancy, incompatible duties and interactions between units. Used alone, it describes a plant that has never been operated.

Bottom-up: operating evidence

SCADA and historian records, laboratory data, operator logs, maintenance events, production records. Data quality screening, percentile and event analysis, empirical envelopes, calibrated models. Finds actual behaviour, intermittent constraints, hidden setpoints, batch cycles and evidence gaps. Used alone, it describes only the conditions the plant has already seen.

Every material claim carries one of the BreakPoint evidence classes, and a claim cannot move up the list without new evidence. We start with the operating record, not the design; we calculate as a check on the record, not a substitute for it.

Method

Six stages. The same five steps.

The work is staged so early findings improve the next round of analysis and the final models stay traceable to plant evidence. The six stages are Frame, Evidence, Reason, Challenge, Decide applied to the capacity question.

01 · Frame

Frame the decision and the service outcome

Planning horizon, required output, quality and licence obligations, operating philosophy, reliability target, planned outages, and the decisions the assessment must support. Which of the five capacities does the decision actually turn on?

Output: scope, decision questions, success criteria, boundaries.
02 · Evidence

Reconstruct the plant and the evidence model

Map the train, sources, parallel units, recycle paths, instruments and laboratory points. Build a governed data model with tag mapping, quality flags, time alignment and lineage. Check the instruments before trusting the data.

Output: process schematic, evidence register, data dictionary, data quality audit.
03 · Evidence, Reason

Establish current operating capability

Characterise flows, loads, water quality, production, operating states and performance. Segment events: backwashes, storms, source changes, shutdowns, high-challenge periods. This is where demonstrated capacity is found.

Output: baseline, event library, operating envelopes, data gaps.
04 · Reason

Model processes, balances and interfaces

Engineering calculations and plant-specific models where useful. Close water and solids balances; test loading, kinetics, contact time, headloss, residuals and recycle; find the coupled constraints unit calculations miss. Reconcile with operators.

Output: unit capacity checks, calibrated models, constraint map, confidence statement.
05 · Challenge

Stress test future and upset scenarios

Demand and load forecasts with seasonal conditions, p95 and p99 water quality, outages, N-1, source blends, control settings and upgrade options. Which assumption is load-bearing? Where is absence of evidence posing as adequacy?

Output: scenario matrix, future capability trajectory, risk thresholds, option sensitivities.
06 · Decide

Prioritise actions and make it reusable

Separate quick operational changes from investigations and capital works. Define staged trials, stop criteria and instrumentation needs. Deploy the logic as a report, dashboard or web tool that can be refreshed as the plant changes.

Output: prioritised roadmap, test plan, business case evidence, reusable decision product.
Three ways to engage

Screen, assess, or run it continuously.

Where the question fits a repeatable pattern we offer a defined scope and deliverable. Bespoke assignments where it doesn’t.

Capacity Screen

One plant or unit · two to four weeks · fixed price

The five capacities, the binding constraint at each edge of the envelope, the evidence class of each, and the evidence gaps. The fastest way to find out whether the number in the planning documents is the informative one.

  • Targeted evidence pack
  • Constraint map
  • Evidence gaps ranked by decision relevance

Capacity Assessment

One plant · detailed · with Evidence or Optimise as needed

Calibrated process models, closed water and solids balances, future and upset scenarios, and a prioritised roadmap that separates operational changes from investigations and capital.

  • Calibrated models and constraint thresholds
  • Scenario matrix and capability trajectory
  • Trial plans with stop criteria; business case evidence

Capacity Programme

Portfolio · recurring · hosted or licensed

The same logic deployed as a secure dashboard or web tool with scheduled data refresh and report generation, across a plant or a portfolio, so the answer updates as demand, assets and water quality change.

  • Governed evidence model and model library
  • Automated reports from the same outputs
  • Capacity module within WaterPoint and AquaPoint
What the approach has done

From our principals’ work.

Delivered in previous roles and not represented as BreakPoint deliveries. Described at pattern level.

  • Portfolio-scale capability. An automated capability assessment workflow, engine and dashboard applied across a major utility’s wastewater plants, first for 27 and now extending to every drinking, recycled and wastewater plant it operates.
  • Filtration capability from the record. A plant-data-calibrated filtration model built from thousands of filter runs, comparing individual filters and separating operational screening from guaranteed capacity.
  • Whole-plant envelope. Source blending, raw water envelopes, taste-and-odour response, clarification, filtration, residuals and net water balance combined with p95 and p99 stress cases and N-1 checks, distinguishing observed production from conditional theoretical headroom.
  • Capability to master plan. Water quality envelopes, demand, SCADA and condition data converted into capacity and bottleneck assessments, prioritised investigations and staged upgrade pathways with decision gates.
  • Deferral through demonstrated headroom. Challenging a business-as-usual upgrade and identifying capital deferral by maximising existing assets: the capacity finding that most often pays for the assessment.
