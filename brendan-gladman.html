Dr Brendan Gladman

Partner, Digital and Data

Chemical engineer and data scientist. Twenty-six years in the water sector, in equal measure across both.

Photo of Dr Brendan Gladman
Career

Process understanding makes a model defensible.

Brendan has spent twenty-six years working on both sides of a line that most of the water industry still treats as a boundary. On the process side: capacity assessment, treatment planning, concept and preliminary design, full-scale trials and plant optimisation. On the data side: machine learning, forecasting and simulation built from SCADA, laboratory and operational data. The combination is the point. Process understanding is what makes a model defensible, and the models are what make engineering judgement quantifiable.

He trained as a chemical engineer at the University of Melbourne and stayed for a PhD on the mathematical modelling of sedimentation and consolidation in flocculated suspensions, with pilot-scale thickener research for CSIRO and AMIRA. That grounding in the physics of solids and liquids runs through everything since, from coagulation control to biosolids forecasting.

After process design with Mott MacDonald inside the Scottish Water Solutions alliance and research management in biofuels and large-scale algal cultivation, he spent five years as a senior process engineer at Melbourne Water. There he converted a 70 ML/d plant from alum to aluminium chlorohydrate, cutting treated-water suspended solids by 80 per cent and operating cost by 20; designed and ran a full-scale pre-filter chlorination trial that took soluble manganese from 0.1 mg/L to below 0.001; initiated the planning for a 600 ML/d UV disinfection upgrade; and introduced machine learning to the utility's process function, forecasting turbidity, clarifier level and filter headloss hours ahead so operators could act before a deviation rather than after it.

As Technical Director for predictive analytics at Jacobs he directed machine learning and digital twin delivery for utilities in Australia, the United States and Singapore, and built the analytics capability across the water business. A digital twin of an Actiflo plant in California held settled water below 1 NTU for six months through model-guided polymer dosing. A four-week-ahead cyanobacteria and taste-and-odour forecast became an operational tool for a Georgia utility. Alongside the analytics he kept doing process investigation, including the root cause of dissolved air flotation pretreatment failure at a Singapore desalination plant, where neither the causal seawater variable nor the mechanism had previously been identified.

At Aurecon he leads digital enablement and water quality for the treatment practice. He built the automated process capacity assessment workflow and web application now applied across Sydney Water's drinking, recycled and wastewater plants, led the process evidence base for Seqwater's pilot asset management master plans, and is process lead for a 250 ML/d plant upgrade from optioneering through business case approval. Operational tools he has built are in daily use for coagulation, chloramination and taste-and-odour control.

At BreakPoint he leads Decision Intelligence and the digital side of the products: the architecture that turns each framework into a deployable engine, the data pipelines that feed it, and the integrations that put BreakPoint outputs inside clients' existing systems. He is co-author of two of the BreakPoint Insights.

Selected projects

Where the data met the process.

A selection from recent and career work. Delivered in previous roles and not represented as BreakPoint Insight deliveries.

Process capacity assessment automation

Sydney Water. Project lead and data engineer, 2022 to present

Automated workflow, Python web application and dashboard for process capacity assessment, first across 27 wastewater plants and now extending to all of Sydney Water's drinking, recycled and wastewater plants.

Pilot asset management master plans

Seqwater, Capalaba and Mt Crosby. Water quality, process and analytics lead, 2025 to present

Integrated water quality envelopes, demand, SCADA and condition data into capacity and bottleneck assessments, prioritised investigations and staged upgrade pathways with decision gates.

Macarthur WFP upgrade, 250 ML/d

Sydney Water. Process lead, 2023 to present

Optioneering through preliminary design, costing and business case approval for new clarification, dosing and organics removal, with models of filtration rate, sludge and chemical use validated against operating data.

Actiflo digital twin

Woodland-Davis, California. Technical director, 2019

Machine learning models trained on plant data set the polymer dose for settled water below 1 NTU across flow and raw-water conditions; held for six months, with significant ozone and polymer savings.

Chloramine decay model and dosing tool

Seqwater. Modelling and data analytics specialist, 2025

Mechanistic modelling and universal differential equations trained on plant data, delivered as an interactive tool that guides operators to the dose set point for a water quality target.

DAF pretreatment root cause

PUB, Tuas Desalination Plant 3, Singapore. Technical advisor, 2019

Why dissolved air flotation stopped removing solids when seawater quality changed: established the causal constituents and mechanism, then a testing programme to restore design performance.

Alum to ACH coagulant conversion

Melbourne Water, Tarago WTP, 70 ML/d. Senior process engineer, 2016 to 2017

Investigated and managed the conversion: 80 per cent reduction in treated-water suspended solids, 20 per cent reduction in operating cost.

Pre-filter chlorination for manganese

Melbourne Water. Senior process engineer, 2017

Concept, feasibility and full-scale trial reducing soluble manganese from 0.1 mg/L to below 0.001 mg/L.

Feed-forward coagulation control

Melbourne Water. Project initiator and model developer, 2018 to 2019

An algorithm extracting features from spectral data, correcting for solids interference and outputting coagulant dose to the plant PLC for a target removal, at Melbourne's largest conventional plant.

Geosmin forecasting

Watercare, Auckland. Machine learning developer, 2023

Empirical dynamic modelling to test causal relationships between water quality, climate and geosmin; the forecast now sets PAC dosing rates.

UV disinfection upgrade, 600 ML/d

Melbourne Water, Winneke WTP. Project initiator, 2015 to 2017

Various utilities. Machine learning developer, 2022 to present

A hybrid mechanistic and machine learning tool that stays interpretable while fitting constants from data across plant types, so utilities can estimate and reduce their emission factor without direct monitoring.
